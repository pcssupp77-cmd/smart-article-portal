When is Ashtami 2026? Dates, Confusion Cleared

News · 16 Oct 2026

Every year, the Hindu calendar brings a series of festivals that are eagerly awaited across India. This year, a wave of online queries about "अष्टमी कब है" (When is Ashtami?) has highlighted a common confusion between the dates of Ashtami and Navami during Sharda Navratri. Below we unpack the recent headlines, explain the calendar mechanics, and outline what devotees can look forward to in the coming days.

What happened

On 16 October 2026, two major Hindi news portals published stories that directly addressed the date dilemma. AajTak ran a piece titled "अष्टमी-नवमी क्या एक ही दिन हैं? 18 या 19 अक्टूबर का कन्फ्यूजन खत्म, जानें सही तारीख," while India TV Hindi echoed the query with "शारदीय नवरात्रि की अष्टमी कब है 18 या 19 अक्टूबर? तुरंत दूर करें कन्फ्यूजन." Both articles clarified that Ashtami and Navami fall on consecutive days in the Sharda Navratri cycle, not on the same day, and provided the exact Gregorian dates for 2026. In a related cultural note, Jagran reported on the age limit for girls participating in the traditional Kanya Puja, adding another layer of festival‑related discussion.

Why it is trending

The surge in searches for "अष्टमी कब है" can be traced to three main factors. First, the lunar‑based Hindu calendar does not align neatly with the Gregorian calendar, leading to year‑to‑year variations that many people find hard to track. Second, media coverage this week highlighted the specific confusion between 18 and 19 October, prompting readers to verify the correct day for Ashtami. Finally, the broader context of Sharda Navratri—a nine‑night celebration dedicated to the goddess Durga—means that devotees are planning rituals, fasting schedules, and community events, all of which depend on precise dates.

Key facts

  • Ashtami 2026 falls on 18 October. This is the eighth day of Sharda Navratri.
  • Navami follows on 19 October. It is the ninth and final day of the Navratri sequence.
  • Both days are observed according to the lunar month of Ashwin, which shifts each year relative to the Gregorian calendar.
  • Media outlets have clarified that Ashtami and Navami are not the same day; they occur on consecutive evenings.
  • Kan​ya Puja, a ritual for young girls, traditionally involves participants up to a certain age, though exact age limits vary by region and are guided by scriptural recommendations.

What to watch next

With Ashtami on 18 October and Navami on 19 October, the immediate focus will shift to the concluding ceremonies of Sharda Navratri, including the traditional Vijayadashami celebrations on 20 October. Devotees can expect processions, cultural performances, and community feasts across major cities. Additionally, reports suggest that regional media will cover the upcoming Durga Puja festivities in West Bengal, which often begin a few days after Navratri ends. Keep an eye on local announcements for timing of special aarti sessions and charitable events that are customary during this period.

FAQ

When exactly is Ashtami in 2026?

Ashtami is on 18 October 2026, the eighth day of the Sharda Navratri cycle.

Is Ashtami the same day as Navami?

No. Ashtami and Navami are consecutive days; Ashtami is on 18 October and Navami follows on 19 October.

What age group can participate in Kan​ya Puja?

Traditional guidelines suggest that young girls up to a certain age—often around 10 to 12 years—may take part, though exact limits differ by community and are based on scriptural advice.
