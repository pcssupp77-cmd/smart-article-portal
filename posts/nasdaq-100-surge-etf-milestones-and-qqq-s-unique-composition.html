Nasdaq 100 Surge: ETF Milestones and QQQ’s Unique Composition

Business · 15 Oct 2026

Introduction

The Nasdaq 100 index has re‑emerged on Indian search feeds, drawing attention from both retail and institutional investors who are keen on the tech‑heavy basket of 100 of the largest non‑financial U.S. companies.

What happened

Recent headlines highlight two distinct developments. South Korea’s KODEX U.S. Nasdaq 100 ETF crossed the 10 trillion‑won mark in assets, according to the Seoul Economic Daily. In parallel, The Globe and Mail and The Motley Fool note that Invesco’s flagship QQQ ETF does not hold a single banking stock, underscoring the index’s exclusion of financial firms.

Why it is trending

Search interest spikes when investors seek clarity on how the Nasdaq 100’s performance may affect their portfolios. The KODEX milestone signals strong demand for passive exposure to U.S. tech, while QQQ’s composition raises questions about sector concentration and risk management. Media coverage amplifies these points, prompting Indian readers to explore the index’s relevance to local markets.

Key facts

  • The Nasdaq 100 comprises the 100 largest non‑financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange.
  • KODEX’s U.S. Nasdaq 100 ETF now holds assets exceeding 10 trillion won (approximately US$7.5 billion).
  • Invesco QQQ’s holdings are limited to non‑financial sectors such as technology, consumer discretionary and health care.
  • Both ETFs aim to replicate the index’s price performance, but they differ in currency exposure and regional investor base.
  • The index’s top constituents include Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta Platforms, which together account for a substantial share of the index’s total market cap.

What to watch next

Analysts suggest keeping an eye on upcoming earnings reports from the Nasdaq 100’s mega‑caps, as well as any regulatory shifts that could affect technology valuations. In addition, currency fluctuations between the won, the U.S. dollar and the Indian rupee may influence the relative attractiveness of foreign‑listed ETFs for Indian investors.

FAQ

What is the Nasdaq 100?

The Nasdaq 100 is a stock market index that tracks the performance of the 100 largest non‑financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange, representing a broad slice of the U.S. technology and growth sectors.

How does an ETF track the Nasdaq 100?

An ETF purchases the underlying stocks in the same proportion as the index, or uses a sampling strategy, to mirror the index’s price movements. Investors then buy shares of the ETF, gaining exposure without buying each constituent individually.

Why doesn’t QQQ own any banks?

By definition, the Nasdaq 100 excludes financial firms, so QQQ, which aims to replicate the index, holds zero banking stocks. This design emphasizes technology and consumer‑focused companies, offering a sector‑biased exposure that some investors prefer for growth‑oriented strategies.
