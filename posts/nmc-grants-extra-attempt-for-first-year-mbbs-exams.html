NMC Grants Extra Attempt for First‑Year MBBS Exams

Education · 16 Oct 2026

Introduction

The National Medical Commission (NMC) has rolled out a one‑time “mercy attempt” for MBBS students who enrolled from the 2019‑20 academic year, giving them an additional chance to clear the first professional examination. The announcement has instantly become a top search on Google Trends in India, reflecting the anxiety and hope of thousands of aspiring doctors.

What happened

Reports from Medical Dialogues, The Telegraph India and Deccan Herald confirm that the NMC will allow a single extra attempt for eligible students who have previously failed the first‑year MBBS exam. The relief measure is confined to candidates admitted in the 2019‑20 batch and later, and it is explicitly described as a one‑off provision rather than a permanent change to the assessment framework. The commission has communicated the decision through official circulars, and medical colleges are expected to coordinate the logistics of the extra exam.

Why it is trending

Searches for “nmc extra attempt first professional mbbs” have surged as the deadline for the mercy attempt draws near. The first professional exam is a critical hurdle; failure can delay graduation by a year and increase financial strain. Media coverage across national dailies and online portals has amplified public interest, turning the policy into a trending topic on Google’s real‑time analytics for India. Students, parents, and educators are actively looking for details on eligibility, application procedures, and the impact on academic calendars.

Key facts

  • The mercy attempt is limited to a single extra try for each eligible student.
  • Only candidates admitted from the 2019‑20 academic session onward are covered.
  • The initiative was announced by the NMC in early October 2026.
  • It applies exclusively to the first professional (first‑year) MBBS examination.
  • Students must have attempted the exam at least once before and meet other criteria set by the NMC.
  • The extra attempt does not alter the overall duration of the MBBS programme.
  • Medical colleges will schedule the mercy exam in coordination with the NMC’s guidelines.
  • Further instructions on documentation, application deadlines and exam dates are expected to be released by individual institutions.

What to watch next

Stakeholders are awaiting a detailed circular from the NMC that will outline the application process, required paperwork, and the exact timeline for the mercy exam. Medical colleges are likely to issue campus‑wide notices, and students should monitor their institution’s portals for updates. Analysts suggest that the outcome of this one‑time attempt could influence future policy discussions about flexibility in medical assessments, especially if a significant number of students succeed on the second try. Additionally, any feedback from the participating colleges may shape whether similar provisions are considered for other years of the MBBS curriculum.

FAQ

Who is eligible for the NMC’s mercy attempt?

Eligibility is currently limited to MBBS students who were admitted from the 2019‑20 academic year onward and have already taken the first professional exam at least once. The NMC may release additional eligibility criteria, such as minimum attendance or academic standing, in its forthcoming guidelines.

How many extra attempts can a student receive?

The policy provides only one additional attempt per eligible student. It is intended as a one‑time relief measure and does not create a recurring right to retake the exam.

Will the extra attempt affect the overall length of the MBBS course?

Reports suggest that the mercy attempt is designed to fit within the existing programme schedule, so it should not extend the total duration of the degree. However, students should confirm the final timetable with their college to avoid any unintended delays.
